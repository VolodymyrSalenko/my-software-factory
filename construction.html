How the Ancient Greeks Built

Further scholarly work on quarrying, stone transport, building accounts and metal fastenings in Greek construction.

Quarrying, transport and labour

Digital survey and mapping with Google Earth: land transport of quarried stone for temple construction at Selinunte

Andrea Samz-Pustol (author name as given in a search summary), Society for Classical Studies annual meeting abstract

Maps the roads used to haul stone to Selinunte's temples and estimates that one column drum took about a day to move, implying 266 day-long trips for Temple E's outer colonnade alone. Hauling was limited by weather and the farming calendar.

Marble in the Mountains: Econometrics of Quarrying and Transporting Building Stones for the Temple of Athena Alea at Tegea, Greece

Jari Pakkanen, in Shaping Cultural Landscapes (Sidestone Press, 2022), pp. 175–185

Reconstructs the stone volume of the fourth-century BC temple from its remains, then applies labour cost rates to estimate quarrying and transport costs, the construction timeframe and the number of workers needed.

Pakkanen 2023 (author final version)

Per a search summary, uses building accounts to study labour rates and the economics of Greek construction, including the Classical shipsheds at Zea. I did not see the title or text, so verify before citing.

Masonry and metal fastenings

Finite Element Analysis of the Parthenon marble block-steel clamp system response under acceleration

Vangelatos, Delagrammatikas, Papadopoulou, Titakis and Vassiliou, Acta IMEKO 10(1) (2021), pp. 155–165

Models how marble blocks joined by steel clamps, set in lead or cement mortar, respond to earthquake-like accelerations. It is mainly a conservation study of the clamp system, and finds that corrosion-induced stress could fracture blocks.
