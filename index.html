Architectural Processes of the Ancient Greeks

A short survey of scholarly work on how Greek temples were designed, built and lifted into place.

1. Design: how temples were planned

Doric Measure and Architectural Design 2: A Modular Reading of the Classical Temple

Mark Wilson Jones, American Journal of Archaeology 105 (2001), pp. 675–713

Argues that a group of temples was laid out with proportional and modular rules, with the triglyph width as the main module, and a foot of about 327 mm.

Ancient Architecture and Mathematics: Methodology and the Doric Temple

Mark Wilson Jones, Springer chapter (2015)

Supports a modular design method resembling the one described by Vitruvius, while noting that Vitruvius's specific method has been discredited. Cites a metrological relief from Salamis as evidence.

Vitruvius's Doric recommendations tested against 63 buildings

Claudio Walter Gomez Duarte, Classica 34(1) (2021), pp. 31–49

Finds that several buildings match Vitruvius's recommendations, but most do not. A related article by the same author reviews Wilson Jones's ten-temple database and judges the modular hypothesis pertinent.

New Approaches to Ancient Greek Architecture

Jean des Courtils, APTHA (open access)

Argues that the techniques were rudimentary yet produced near-perfect results, and distinguishes simple buildings from ones built to a pre-established plan with elaborate geometric proportions.

2. Construction: lifting, joining and roofing

Early lifting devices on Greek temples (Isthmia and Corinth)

Alessandro Pierattini, Annual of the British School at Athens (2019); link is the publisher's blog summary

Twin grooves on the undersides of 200–400 kg blocks (c. 700–650 BC) are read as rope channels for a frame-based lifting device, suggesting lifting machines about 150 years earlier than the conventional date for the crane. The interpretation is by a single author and still debated.

Wooden roof structures of the Temple of Apollo at Delphi

French doctoral thesis (HAL archive)

Uses building accounts, 3D modelling and structural calculation, and concludes that Greek carpenters of the 6th–5th centuries BC likely used triangulated roof structures.

Greek temple construction

EBSCO research overview (not peer-reviewed)

Describes the sequence from quarrying and rough trimming to transport. Blocks were carved on every face for tight joints and held mainly by friction, with iron clamps and lead-set dowels as backup.

Common threads

  • Scholars broadly agree that Greek architects used arithmetic proportions and modules, but disagree on the exact method.
  • Building technology was simple, but the results were precise.
  • Lifting and roofing techniques appear earlier and more sophisticated than once believed.
