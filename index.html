Why did it happen?

Three pressures built up before 1789.

Unequal society

Clergy and nobles had privileges. Almost everyone else, the Third Estate, paid most of the taxes.

Empty treasury

Wars and court spending left the crown deep in debt. About half of the royal budget went on debt interest.

Hungry people

Bad harvests pushed bread prices up. By mid-1789 a loaf cost about half as much again as a year earlier.

Three Estates, one vote each

At the Estates-General of 1789, how votes were counted decided everything.

    Four people to know

    Their lives were tied to the Revolution.

    Louis XVI

    1754–1793

    King of France. He called the Estates-General, tried to flee in 1791, and was executed in 1793.

    Marie Antoinette

    1755–1793

    Queen and wife of Louis XVI. Widely distrusted for her Austrian birth; guillotined in October 1793.

    Maximilien Robespierre

    1758–1794

    Lawyer and leading member of the Committee of Public Safety during the Terror. Executed after Thermidor.

    Napoleon Bonaparte

    1769–1821

    Army general who seized power in 1799 and later crowned himself emperor.
