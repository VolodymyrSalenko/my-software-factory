How Greek Architects Designed Buildings

Four further sources on proportion, Vitruvius, optical refinements and drawing methods.

Design: proportion, refinements and drawings

The operational concepts in the Vitruvian system of design

Archai (journal), 2019; author not identified in the search results

Argues that Vitruvian symmetria draws on two kinds of relation: modular (commodulatio) and dimensional (commensus). It describes symmetria as an ideal state in which elements keep dimensional or geometric relationships to one another. The article is in Portuguese.

Entasis experiment (exact title not confirmed)

Thompson, Papadopoulou and Vassiliou, Spatial Vision 20(6) (2007), pp. 531–543

Tests experimentally whether column entasis compensates for an optical illusion. Finds little support for illusion-compensation theories, nor for a purely aesthetic motive, and presents evidence for an engineering role.

The Art of Building in the Classical World: Vision, Craftsmanship, and Linear Perspective in Greek and Roman Architecture

John R. Senseney, Cambridge University Press (book; year not confirmed in the results)

Argues that Greek graphic construction shaped how buildings were designed. It analyses the Didyma wall drawings and proposes a hypothetical working drawing for the Parthenon's curvature.

Parthenon curves: optical illusion or not? (Alain Goriely's challenge)

Smithsonian Magazine news report on Goriely, Royal Society Open Science (recent); the link is the news report, not the paper

Reports the argument that the visual effects usually cited for the Parthenon's curvature are unproven or too weak to notice at normal viewing distances. I did not find a direct URL to the paper itself.
